**Goal.** Quantify how much capital each strategy can absorb before transaction
costs eat its edge. Hold each book's pre-cost target weights fixed, scale to every
GMV on a log grid ($1M … $2B), and push the trades through the house cost model
(5&nbsp;bps spread + ADV-calibrated impact, calibrated so a 1%-of-ADV trade costs
10&nbsp;bps). Report the **break-even GMV** (net alpha → 0) and **half-capacity
GMV** (net Sharpe → ½ of frictionless), decompose cost drag into spread vs impact,
and stress the impact exponent.

The per-dollar cost of a rebalance splits cleanly into a size-invariant spread term
and a participation-growing impact term:

$$
\text{cost} \;=\; \underbrace{s \cdot \tau}_{\text{spread}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\eta \left(\frac{q}{\mathrm{ADV}}\right)^{\gamma}}_{\text{impact}}
$$

where $\tau$ is one-way turnover, $q$ the per-name trade, and $\gamma$ the impact
exponent. Books are each run at their net-optimal rebalance frequency (from the
earlier decay/combination projects): **Momentum** (12-1, 2-weekly), the
**Combined** IR-composite (weekly), and short-term **Reversal** (1-week signal,
daily).

## 1. Headline: gross edge and capacity are *inversely* related

| Strategy | Gross Sharpe | Gross alpha | Break-even GMV | Half-capacity GMV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Momentum (2W) | 0.117 | 1.13%/yr | **$135M** | $28M |
| Combined (weekly) | 0.349 | 2.89%/yr | $19M | < $1M |
| Reversal-1w (daily) | 0.517 | 4.41%/yr | **~$0** | < $1M |

**The strategy with the most attractive gross returns has essentially no capacity,
and the least attractive has the most.** Reversal has the highest gross Sharpe
(0.52) but is **spread-dominated** — its 8.6%/yr spread drag alone (from ~86×
annual one-way turnover) exceeds its 4.4% gross alpha, so it loses money net at
*any* tradeable size. Momentum's slow 12-1 signal turns over little, so its thin
1.1% gross alpha survives to a **$135M** book. The combined book sits between: a
healthy 0.35 gross Sharpe, but its faster signals push turnover up and cap it at
~$19M.

![Net Sharpe and net alpha versus GMV for the three books, with break-even and half-capacity GMV marked.](@/assets/research/capacity-curves/net_curves.png)

This is the capacity paradox every systematic PM lives with: **decay speed, not
gross Sharpe, sets scalability.** A slow signal with a mediocre Sharpe is worth
more AUM than a fast signal with a great one.

## 2. Cost decomposition — spread is flat, impact bends

At each GMV the drag splits into a **spread** component (invariant to size — it
depends only on turnover) and an **impact** component (grows with participation).
For the slow momentum book, spread drag is a trivial 0.42%/yr and impact only
overtakes gross alpha past $100M. For reversal, spread drag is 8.6%/yr — the book
is dead on arrival, before impact even matters. The figures below show the
gross-alpha line (dashed) and the point where the spread+impact stack crosses it
(the break-even GMV).

![Cost decomposition for the momentum book: spread stays flat while impact grows with GMV, crossing gross alpha near $135M.](@/assets/research/capacity-curves/cost_decomp_momentum.png)

![Cost decomposition for the reversal book: spread drag alone exceeds gross alpha at every size.](@/assets/research/capacity-curves/cost_decomp_reversal1w.png)

Per-name participation at the ceiling shows momentum comfortably under the 10%-ADV
cap at $135M, while the fast books would blow through it well before reaching
meaningful size.

![Histogram of per-name participation at the capacity ceiling for each book.](@/assets/research/capacity-curves/participation_hist.png)

## 3. Impact-model sensitivity — capacity is model-dependent for fast books

Re-running with the Almgren **square-root law** — impact $\propto \sqrt{q/\mathrm{ADV}}$,
i.e. $\gamma = \tfrac12$ — instead of the house quadratic-in-trade model:

| Strategy | Break-even (quadratic) | Break-even (√-law) |
|---|---|---|
| Momentum (2W) | $134.7M | $134.3M |
| Combined (weekly) | $18.7M | **$2.7M** |
| Reversal-1w | ~$0 | ~$0 |

**Momentum's capacity is model-robust** (~$135M either way) because it trades near
the 1%-ADV calibration point where the two laws agree. **The combined book's
capacity is highly model-dependent** — the √-law, which is steeper at the small
participations the book actually trades, cuts its break-even by 7× to $2.7M. The
lesson: capacity numbers for fast, small-trade books carry large model risk and
should be quoted as ranges, not points; the quadratic model is optimistic there.

![Break-even GMV under the quadratic versus square-root impact laws, per strategy.](@/assets/research/capacity-curves/impact_sensitivity.png)

## 4. Honest limitations

1. **Pre-cost target books, not the traded books.** Capacity is measured on the
   fixed alpha target (full rebalance to target at each date). The combined
   strategy is actually traded through a cost-penalised QP whose endogenous
   no-trade region *raises* capacity — this study measures the raw signal's
   scalability; the QP is the mitigation.
2. **Single-name ADV impact, no cross-impact or dynamics.** Real execution spreads
   trades over time and across correlated names; the static model is a first-order
   estimate.
3. **Small gross alphas.** These are unlevered dollar-neutral books on 200 large
   caps; the *shape* and *ordering* of the capacity curves are the robust story,
   not the precise dollar ceilings.
